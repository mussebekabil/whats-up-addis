Meskel Square is the beating heart of Addis Ababa — a vast open plaza that serves as the city's primary venue for major public events, national celebrations, and religious ceremonies. Covering several hectares in the centre of the capital, the square is named after the Ethiopian Orthodox Christian holiday of Meskel (meaning "Cross"), which commemorates the finding of the True Cross by Empress Helena in the 4th century.

## The Meskel Celebration

The highlight of the year at Meskel Square is the Demera bonfire ceremony, held on the eve of the Meskel holiday (usually 26–27 September). Tens of thousands of worshippers dressed in traditional white garments gather to watch the ceremonial lighting of a massive pyramid-shaped bonfire decorated with yellow Meskel daisies. The Archbishop of the Ethiopian Orthodox Church leads the proceedings, and the direction the bonfire falls is said to foretell the year ahead. The smoke and flames rise dramatically into the night sky — it is one of the most spectacular events in Ethiopia.

## Year-Round Significance

Beyond religious celebrations, Meskel Square has historically hosted major political rallies, military parades, New Year festivities (Enkutatash), and public gatherings. The square can accommodate hundreds of thousands of people at its largest events.

## The Surrounding Area

The square is flanked by the Addis Ababa City Hall, several embassies, and key government buildings. The area is a hub of activity throughout the day, with vendors, commuters, and sightseers filling the surrounding streets. The nearby Meskel Flower neighbourhood offers a wealth of restaurants, cafes, and shops.

## Visitor Tips

- The square is most impressive during major celebrations — plan your visit around Meskel (September/October) or Timkat (January).
- The area is generally safe during daylight hours; exercise normal urban caution at night.
- A great vantage point for photographing Addis Ababa's skyline.
