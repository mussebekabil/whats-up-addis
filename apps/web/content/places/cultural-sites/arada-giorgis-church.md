Arada Giorgis — St. George's Cathedral — is one of the most historically significant churches in Addis Ababa and one of Ethiopia's most important places of worship. Founded by Emperor Menelik II in **1896** to celebrate the stunning Ethiopian victory at the **Battle of Adwa** — in which Ethiopia decisively defeated an invading Italian army — the church is named for St. George, the patron saint of soldiers and warriors, whose intercession Menelik attributed to the victory.

The church holds a unique place in Ethiopian history: it was here that **Emperor Haile Selassie was crowned** on 2 November 1930, an event attended by dignitaries from around the world and celebrated globally. That coronation would later inspire the founding of the Rastafari faith, making Arada Giorgis a site of global spiritual significance.

## What to See

### The Cathedral Exterior

The church's octagonal plan — traditional for Ethiopian Orthodox churches — is crowned with a striking dome and surrounded by well-kept grounds. The exterior walls display colourful murals of Ethiopian saints and biblical scenes, a visual feast that draws the eye at every turn.

### The Interior

The interior of the church is adorned with elaborate paintings, embroidered ceremonial curtains (habesha fabric), gilded artifacts, and icons of St. George slaying the dragon — the most common image in Ethiopian Orthodox art. The atmosphere during morning prayers, with incense smoke curling through shafts of coloured light, is deeply moving.

### The Coronation Hall

A section of the church preserves artifacts and commemorates the historic 1930 coronation of Emperor Haile Selassie I. Historical photographs and ceremonial objects are on display, offering a tangible connection to one of Ethiopia's most celebrated moments.

### St. George's Day

The feast day of St. George (Kidus Giorgis) is celebrated with particular fervour at this church. Thousands of white-robed worshippers crowd the compound; the ceremonies, chanting, and processions are among the most spectacular religious observances in Addis Ababa.

## Piassa Neighbourhood

Arada Giorgis sits in the heart of **Piassa**, Addis Ababa's oldest commercial district, which retains much of its early 20th-century Italian-influenced architecture. After visiting the church, explore the neighbourhood's historic cafés, bookshops, and artisan stores for a full sense of old Addis.

## Visitor Tips

- Dress modestly — women should cover their hair and both men and women should cover arms and legs.
- Shoes should be removed at the church entrance.
- Services in Ge'ez are conducted in the early morning; arrive by 6:30 AM for the most immersive experience.
- Photography inside the sanctuary is generally not permitted; respectfully check before taking photos.
- The church is easily combined with a visit to Tewodros Square, which is nearby.
