Menagesha Suba Forest Reserve is one of Africa's oldest protected forests, located roughly 40 kilometres west of Addis Ababa on the slopes of Mount Wachacha. Established as a royal hunting preserve by Emperor Zara Yaqob in the 15th century — making it one of the world's earliest forest conservation efforts — Menagesha Suba is today a state forest reserve and a biodiversity hotspot within easy reach of the capital.

## The Forest

### Ancient Afromontane Ecosystem

The forest is dominated by podocarpus (yellowwood), hagenia, and wanza trees, many of them centuries old. Dense canopy coverage creates a cathedral-like environment on the forest floor, where tree ferns, mosses, and orchids grow in the cool, misted air. Walking through Menagesha feels like stepping into a primeval forest that has barely changed in a thousand years.

### Wildlife

Menagesha Suba provides refuge for colobus monkeys, olive baboons, servals, mongooses, and over 200 bird species. The forest is particularly rewarding for birders, with the Ethiopian siskin, Abyssinian woodpecker, and white-cheeked turaco among the endemic and near-endemic species recorded here.

## Activities

### Forest Hiking

Well-marked trails range from short loops near the forest entrance to full-day hikes to the summit of Mount Wachacha (3,386 m), from where views extend across the Addis Ababa plain on clear days.

### Bird Watching

The forest is considered one of the best birding sites within reach of Addis Ababa. Guided birding walks can be arranged through the reserve management.

### Camping

Basic camping facilities are available for visitors who wish to spend the night in the forest and experience the dawn chorus — a symphony of birdsong that must be heard to be believed.

## Visitor Tips

- Hire a local guide from the forest station — they know the trails and wildlife.
- The forest is significantly cooler and wetter than Addis; bring layers and rain gear.
- Access by private vehicle or shared minibus from Ambo road.
- Visit midweek for a quieter experience.
